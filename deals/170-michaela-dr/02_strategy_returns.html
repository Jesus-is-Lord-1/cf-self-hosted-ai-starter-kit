Underwriting · Strategy & Returns · July 2026

Four ways to play 170 Michaela Drive — and the one that survives the math

Every strategy below was underwritten with the same sourced inputs: mid-2026 financing, non-homestead taxes, real comps, and no assumed rate cuts. Two strategies work at the right basis, one is rejected on arithmetic, one is a negotiation fallback.

The verdict: offer $1,750,000. Walk away above ~$1,800,000.

Run Strategy A (staged resale) as the primary track with Strategy B (furnished executive lease into a spring 2027 exit) as the built-in fallback if not under contract by month four. At a $1.75M basis the all-in cost is ~$375/sqft against a $420–470/sqft comp band — the profit is bought at acquisition, not hoped for at exit. At the current $2.15M ask, no strategy clears a professional hurdle: the correct move at that price is to pass.

Deal lifecycle · financing at every stage

How the deal is financed from entry to exit

Stage 1 · Acquisition
Private bridge loan

65% LTC at ~9.5% interest-only + 2 pts, 12-month term (Atlanta market: 9.5–10.2%). Close in 21 days — speed and certainty are the negotiating currency against a fatigued seller.

Stage 2 · Operation
Carry management

Taxes ~$20K/yr (lagged assessment), insurance ~$12K, utilities $750/mo. Strategy B adds a furnished executive lease at ~$8,500/mo to offset ~70% of carry.

Stage 3 · Disposition
Retail sale — primary exit

Relist inside the comp band at $430/sqft (~$2.2M) where this submarket clears in 1–2.5 months. Exit buyer financing: jumbo at ~6.6%, a 7-week low — affordability tailwind.

Stage 3-alt · Refinance
Tested — and rejected

75% cash-out at 7.75% needs ~$119K/yr of debt service against ~$41K furnished NOI (0.34 DSCR). The honest Plan B is a price cut to the $2.0–2.05M comp floor, which the bear case already prices in.

Hold 6–10 months · bridge 65% LTC · $25K staging · equity ≈ $780–810K

Buy the discount, fix the marketing. The house doesn't need work — it needs a price inside the comp band, professional staging, and a broker who isn't the builder. List at ~$2.2M ($430/sqft), directly on top of the 477 Michael Dr precedent that cleared in 55 days one street over.

Strategy C · Rejected
Rejected on arithmetic — shown for discipline

Buy, hold, refinance as a luxury rental

Tested at the $2.15M ask with mid-2026 DSCR terms

Strategy D · Negotiation fallback
Deploy only if the seller won't move on price

Creative structure: lease-option or seller carry

Trades our price for the seller's terms — costs almost no capital to propose

The builder is likely paying 9–13% on a construction loan. If they refuse $1.75M cash, offer terms that solve their carry problem while capping our basis:

  • Lease-option: 24-month option at a $1.85M strike; ~$8,500/mo lease with $1,500/mo credited toward purchase. We control the asset for ~$25K of option money instead of ~$800K of equity, and exercise only if the spring market confirms the exit.
  • Seller carry: $1.90M price with the builder holding a 75% note at 5–6% interest-only for 18 months. The above-market price is financed below our bridge cost, roughly offsetting itself; the builder converts dead carry into interest income.

These are presented as negotiation instruments, not the plan of record — returns depend on final terms and are bounded by the same comp band as Strategies A and B.
