Verdict: Real discount, inflated ARV — pursue, but underwrite to ~$2.5M

This is a 2025-built new-construction home near downtown Alpharetta, not a classic rehab. It last traded as a teardown for $570K in Oct 2024, was built out, then listed Aug 2025 at $2,695,000, cut to $2,495,000, and delisted Sept 25, 2025 without selling. It has cycled through multiple MLS numbers since. That history is the single most important fact in this deal:

Red flag on the $3.0M ARV. The open market already declined this exact house at $2,495,000 ($487/sq ft) in fall 2025, and Alpharetta prices have since drifted down ~5.4% YoY (median $724K, Mar 2026) with days-on-market rising (43 vs 36). A $3.0M exit is ~20% above a price that already failed. Do not underwrite to it without a same-street, same-size closed comp.
Why the deal still works. At $1.82M you are buying 27% below the failed list price and ~$355/sq ft — well under replacement cost for finished luxury product in 30009. Even at a conservative $2.5M exit, the model shows ~$294K profit all-cash (14.3% ROI, ~8 mo) or ~$154K financed (~27% cash-on-cash). The margin of safety comes from the entry price, not the ARV story.

What the tape says

  • The broad Alpharetta market is cooling, not crashing. Median prices are off ~5% YoY with DOM stretching; well-priced homes in premium pockets still see competitive offers, but stale or aspirational pricing sits.
  • Downtown 30009 is the strongest micro-location in the city — walkability to Main Street, Avalon proximity, the Alpha Loop, and top Fulton schools (Milton HS feeder pattern). New luxury projects (Ronara Villas, Mayfair on Main, Byers Park) are proving $1.6M+ demand steps from downtown, and they are also this flip's direct competition.
  • The $2.5M–$3M band in 30009 is thin. Most volume clears below $1.6M; buyers at $2.5M+ compare against Milton estates on acreage. That means longer marketing time and appraisal risk at the top of the range.
  • This specific asset has price discovery already: $2.695M (rejected) → $2.495M (rejected) → delisted → multiple relists. A stale, multi-MLS history means the buyer pool has seen it; the relaunch must look and feel like a different product (new photography, staging, completed punch list, fresh narrative).

3 · Strategy & Action Plan

Offer & negotiation

  • Open at $1.65M, target ≤ $1.72M, walk above $1.82M. Leverage: 13+ months of failed marketing, price cut, delist, multiple relists, −5% market, and your speed/certainty of close. Every $50K off entry ≈ $50K straight to profit and drops breakeven by ~$53K of exit price.
  • If the seller won't move on price, move on terms: seller completes punch-list items or credits at closing; seller pays transfer costs; 45-day free look with access for contractors.
  • If this is a wholesaler assignment, ask for the contract price. At a $1.82M ask against a probable sub-$1.7M contract, negotiate the spread or go direct after expiry.

Diligence checklist (before hard money, non-negotiable)

  • City of Alpharetta permits & CO: confirm certificate of occupancy status and any open/expired permits. If no CO, get a written path-to-CO estimate — this is where $173K becomes $300K.
  • Title & liens: full exam for mechanic's liens, materialman claims, builder LLC judgments; require lien waivers/affidavits at closing.
  • Independent scope validation: your own GC bids the $173K scope line-by-line; structural + stucco/water-intrusion inspection on the new build (new ≠ sound).
  • ARV proof: licensed agent CMA of 30009/Milton closed sales $2.2–3.0M (last 6 mo) + walk the active competition (Ronara Villas, Mayfair on Main).
  • Survey, HOA (if any), flood cert, insurance quote before the diligence clock runs.
